# Player DNA feature dictionary

## Purpose and source

Generated by Scripts/create_player_dna.py from data/player_profiles.csv.
The current vector describes chess-engine-measured performance tendencies,
not a complete or validated representation of playing style. It contains no
game outcomes, ratings, player/game identifiers as numeric inputs, or
color-specific metrics.

The data has 30 players with 40 games each. Features are evaluated over the
existing cohort. The normalization parameters are saved in
data/player_dna_scaler_parameters.csv so later profiles can be transformed
with the same medians and IQRs.

## Included features

| Feature | Meaning and calculation | Expected range | Normalization | Chess interpretation and reason | Limitations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| median_cpl | Median of the player's 40 game-level average centipawn-loss values. Each game average includes only centipawn-evaluable moves; mate-score comparisons are excluded. | Nonnegative centipawns. Observed: 35.005–112.065. | dna_median_cpl = (raw value − cohort median 70.680) / cohort IQR 28.72625. Lower raw CPL indicates less average engine-evaluated loss. | Robust summary of typical move quality; median reduces sensitivity to unusually poor games. Included as the main overall performance measure. | CPL is engine/depth dependent and does not directly capture strategic style. |
| cpl_std | Sample standard deviation (ddof=1) of the player's 40 per-game average CPL values. | Nonnegative centipawns. Observed: 19.534–52.116. | dna_cpl_std = (raw value − cohort median 29.3934) / cohort IQR 9.51834. Lower indicates more consistent game-level CPL. | Measures how much move-quality performance varies between games. Included as a consistency dimension distinct from typical CPL. | Two IQR outliers occur in this feature; RobustScaler reduces their effect. Variation may reflect opponent and position differences, not only player consistency. |
| inaccuracies_per_100_cp_moves | Total source-classified inaccuracies divided by total centipawn-evaluable moves, multiplied by 100. Source classifies inaccuracies as CPL >10 and <50. Mate-evaluation moves are excluded from both numerator and denominator. | Rate from 0 to 100 per 100 CP-evaluable moves. Observed: 19.372–26.240. | dna_inaccuracies_per_100_cp_moves = (raw value − cohort median 22.8946) / cohort IQR 2.36111. Lower means fewer moderate-error moves per 100 comparable moves. | Describes the frequency of moderate errors, complementing typical CPL and game-to-game variability. Included as an error-profile feature. | Threshold-based classification; strongly dependent on engine depth and source thresholds. The feature is moderately correlated with median CPL (r≈−0.62) and should be interpreted jointly. |

The normalized value is centered at the cohort median and divided by the
interquartile range (IQR). It is dimensionless; values can be negative or
positive. Negative means below the cohort median. The transform does not
reverse direction: lower normalized CPL and inaccuracy rate are better, while
lower normalized CPL standard deviation means greater consistency.

## Excluded features

| Excluded feature group | Reason for exclusion |
|---|---|
| Mean/median accuracy | Accuracy is a custom percentage of CP-evaluable moves below 50 CPL. Mean and median accuracy correlate 0.98; mean accuracy correlates −0.92 with median CPL. Including accuracy with CPL and error rates would over-weight the same performance dimension. |
| Mean/weighted CPL and blunders per 100 moves | Weighted CPL correlates 0.96 with median CPL and 0.98 with blunder rate; blunder rate correlates 0.93 with median CPL. Median CPL is retained as the more robust typical-game measure. |
| Mistake and best-move rates | Move classes are partitions of centipawn-evaluable moves: best/near-best, inaccuracy, mistake, and blunder. Their rates are compositional and should not all be treated as independent dimensions. The selected moderate-error rate is retained as a distinct error-profile descriptor. |
| Ratings and rating difference | These describe estimated playing strength and opponent context, not playing behavior. They may be used separately for strength compatibility, not style similarity. |
| Wins, draws, losses, score rates | These describe results, not style. They can directly leak the outcome when profiles and target results come from the same games. |
| White/Black profile fields | Color-specific exposure is highly uneven; two players have no Black games, and another has only three White games. These measurements are not comparable enough for the shared vector. |
| Move count / game length | Primarily describes game duration and termination, with color/opponent effects. It is not a defensible standalone style feature. |
| Mate-event metrics | Rare and dependent on positions reaching engine-detected mate scores. Retain them descriptively until reliability is assessed on a larger sample. |
| Best/worst game values and game IDs | Extremes are sample-sensitive; IDs are audit references, not player characteristics. |
| Opening, middlegame, endgame, tactical motifs, positional style, aggressiveness | Not available from the current dataset. No phase, motif, or positional features were fabricated. |

## Leakage and future use

The three selected columns are game-performance descriptors, not result
labels. They are suitable as a descriptive comparison foundation, but this
dataset does not provide a held-out temporal period or sufficient players to
claim generalization. For future predictive work, compute profiles from
historical/training games only and evaluate on later or held-out games.

The 30-player cohort is small. EDA correlations are exploratory and can be
unstable. The feature vector should be revisited when more players and
position-level features are available. This file does not specify or implement
a similarity or matchmaking algorithm.
